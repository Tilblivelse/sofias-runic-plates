# Deerness plate

Found at the Brough of Deerness on Mainland Orkney, this is a copper plate of type A. Archaeological evidence places it in 1000–1150 (lV).

No interpretation of the inscription is given in the book.

## Quick facts

- **Catalogue no.:** 1
- **SRD:** Or 21
- **Other signature:** none listed
- **Found:** 2009, Brough of Deerness, parish of St Andrews and Deerness, Mainland Orkney, Scotland, British Isles
- **Context:** domestic (fortification)
- **Object:** type A; copper; 65 mm long, 9-10 mm wide, 1 mm thick

## Runes and layout

- **Rune forms:** conventional and unconventional runes
- **Layout:** two sides, divided into four rows; two rows on each side
- **Reading order:** boustrophedon
- **Punctuation:** possible marks of punctuation in the form of two diagonal strokes
- **Decoration:** no

## Where it is now

- **Current location:** McDonald Institute for Archaeological Research, Cambridge; find no: 3052
- **Examined by Pereswetoff-Morath:** once
